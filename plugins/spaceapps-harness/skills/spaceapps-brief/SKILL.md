# Space Apps ground truth

Load this before answering anything about how the challenge works. Verify dates against
`spaceappschallenge.org` each season - the structure is stable, the dates move.

## What it is

The NASA International Space Apps Challenge is the largest annual global hackathon.
NASA's Science Mission Directorate funds it through a contract with Booz Allen Hamilton,
Mindgrub and SecondMuse. Teams solve published challenges using open data from NASA and
partner space agencies over one weekend. Free, all ages, no skill floor.

Scale: 450+ local events, 190+ countries and territories, 17 space agency partners.

## Rules that bite

- Teams are capped at **six** members, all registered for the **same event**.
- One person, one team. One team, one challenge.
- **No work before the hackathon opens.** Research the domain, set up accounts, learn
  the tools - but do not build.
- Submissions in English.
- A project must be submitted to receive a certificate or be eligible for judging.
- Minors may compete with parental consent, but a minor's name, voice or likeness
  cannot appear in the video or project submission.
- Three event types: in-person local, virtual local, and the **Universal Event** for
  anyone without a local option. Universal Event projects are judged by Universal Event
  judges and feed the same global funnel.

## The judging funnel

Five criteria, applied at every stage:

**Impact** - how much impact, quality and quantity; big problem or little problem.
**Creativity** - how innovative; novel or already attempted.
**Validity** - scientifically valid; will it do what it claims; will it work in the
real world.
**Relevance** - responsive to the challenge; complete or a long way to go.
**Presentation** - how well the team communicated; effective storytelling.

Stages: local judges nominate from their event → global judges (NASA and partner agency
experts) cut nominees to finalists and honorable mentions → an executive panel picks
ten winners, one per award category.

2025 actuals: 114,000+ participants, 551 events, 167 countries, 11,500+ submissions →
1,290+ nominees → 45 finalists + 23 honorable mentions → 10 winners.

Implied odds: roughly 11% of submissions become nominees, roughly 3.5% of nominees
become finalists, and roughly 1 in 1,150 submissions wins globally. Treat "nominee" as
the realistic first target.
