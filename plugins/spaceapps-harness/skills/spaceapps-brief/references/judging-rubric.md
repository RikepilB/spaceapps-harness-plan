# Judging rubric and award categories

## The five criteria, as published

| Criterion | Question judges ask |
|---|---|
| Impact | How much impact (quality and quantity) can this project have? Does it solve a big problem or a little problem? |
| Creativity | How creative/innovative is the approach? Is the project novel and something that hasn't been attempted before? |
| Validity | Is the solution scientifically valid? Will it do what it sets out to do? Can it work in the real world? |
| Relevance | Is this project responsive to the challenge for which it was submitted? Is it a complete solution or does it have a long way to go? |
| Presentation | How well did the team communicate their project? Were they effective in telling the story? |

These have been stable since at least 2019.

## What each stage actually reads

**Local judges** usually see a live or recorded pitch and can ask questions. Presentation
and Relevance dominate, because they have minutes per team.

**Global judges** - NASA and partner-agency subject-matter experts - read the project
page and watch the video, often without the team present. Validity and Impact rise,
because a domain expert can evaluate the method, and nobody is there to clarify.

**The executive panel** picks one winner per award category from the finalists. At this
stage the question is category fit: which project best embodies *this specific award*.

Practical consequence: a project that pitches well locally but has a thin project page
dies at the global stage. Write the page for a reader who will never meet you.

## The ten award categories, as published

| Award | Official definition |
|---|---|
| Best Use of Science | The project that makes the best and most valid use of science and/or the scientific method. |
| Best Use of Data | The project that best makes space data accessible or leverages it for a unique application. |
| Best Use of Technology | The project that exemplifies the most innovative use of technology. |
| Galactic Impact | The project with the most potential to improve life on Earth or in the universe. |
| Best Mission Concept | The project with the most plausible concept and design. |
| Most Inspirational | The project that captures our hearts. |
| Best Use of Storytelling | The project that most creatively communicates the potential of open data through the art of storytelling. |
| Global Connection | The project that best connects people around the world through technology. |
| Art & Technology | The project that most effectively combines technical and creative skills. |
| Local Impact | The project that demonstrates the greatest potential for local impact. |

## Reading the categories as strategy

Ten awards, one project each, so the categories are effectively ten different games:

- **Best Use of Science** rewards method. Show the hypothesis, the test, the control.
- **Best Use of Data** rewards making data usable by someone who could not use it
  before, or combining sources nobody combined.
- **Best Use of Technology** rewards the technique itself. Novel architecture, unusual
  hardware, a genuinely hard engineering problem solved.
- **Galactic Impact** rewards scale of consequence. Needs a credible path from demo to
  deployment.
- **Best Mission Concept** rewards plausibility and systems design over working code.
  The only category where a well-argued concept beats a running app.
- **Most Inspirational** rewards emotional truth. Usually won by teams with a personal
  or community stake in the problem.
- **Best Use of Storytelling** is the most winnable category for a team with a designer
  or writer, and the one engineering teams systematically ignore.
- **Global Connection** rewards projects that link regions - often by combining NASA
  data with partner-agency data, or by shipping in more than one language.
- **Art & Technology** rewards craft. Games, illustration, sonification, animation.
- **Local Impact** rewards specificity of place. A named community, a named problem,
  evidence you understand it.

## The counterintuitive read

The five non-engineering categories - Most Inspirational, Best Use of Storytelling,
Global Connection, Art & Technology, Local Impact - are half the prize pool and attract
a fraction of the technically strong teams. A team with both engineering and narrative
capability should aim at a category where its narrative half is the differentiator, not
at Best Use of Technology where it competes with every strong engineering team at once.

Declare the target category internally at hour 2 and shape the project toward it. Do
not announce it in the submission - you are not asked to nominate a category, and
judges assign them.
