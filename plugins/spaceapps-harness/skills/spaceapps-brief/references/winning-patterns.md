# Winning patterns

Extracted from five years of global winners and the full 2025 finalist and honorable
mention set (45 + 23 projects). Treat as base rates, not laws.

## The recurring project shape

Nearly every winner is the same four-part object:

**(a real, named dataset) + (a transform that does real work) + (an interface a
non-expert can drive) + (a named beneficiary)**

Projects missing part three fail Presentation. Projects missing part four fail Impact.
Projects where part two is a thin wrapper over an existing tool fail Creativity.

## Six patterns that repeat

**1. Translation, not discovery.** Winners rarely find something new in the data. They
make existing data usable by someone who could not use it before. SkySense turned Earth
observation into "will it rain on my picnic". Landsat Connect turned reflectance data
into a notification. The value added is accessibility.

**2. Fusion across sources.** Combining a NASA source with a partner-agency source, or
satellite data with ground truth, is over-represented among winners. It is also the
mechanical route to the Global Connection award. QUEÑARIS combined satellite data,
drones, microorganisms and AI.

**3. Named place, named person.** Local Impact and Most Inspirational both go to
projects with a specific community. Generic global framing loses to "this valley, this
crop, these growers".

**4. The interface is the project.** Judges score what they can perceive. Dashboards,
maps, games and visual narratives dominate. Pure model or pure analysis projects appear
far less often among winners than their share of submissions.

**5. Narrative and art are underserved.** Storytelling, Art & Technology and Most
Inspirational account for three of ten awards, and attract a much thinner field of
technically strong teams. HerCode Space won Storytelling with illustration and
narrative over NASA heliophysics. Zumorroda-X won Art & Technology with mini-games.

**6. Honest limits read as rigour.** Winning descriptions state what the project does
*not* do. Judges scoring Validity treat a named limitation as evidence of competence.

## Geography of the 2025 results

Across the 68 finalists and honorable mentions:

| Origin | Count |

| Universal Event | 11 |
| Brazil | 9 |
| India | 5 |
| Egypt | 5 |
| Bangladesh | 4 |
| Philippines | 3 |
| United States | 3 |
| South Korea | 3 |
| Taiwan, Colombia, Peru, Mexico, Argentina, Turkey | 2 each |
| Sri Lanka, Malaysia, Spain, Ethiopia, Germany, Morocco, Kyrgyzstan, UAE, France, Canada, Italy, Saudi Arabia, Vietnam | 1 each |

Two readings worth holding at once. The Universal Event is the single largest source of
finalists, which argues it is not a weaker route. And the Global South is
over-represented relative to naive expectations - Brazil alone produced more finalists
than the United States - which suggests local stake and specificity travel further with
judges than institutional resources do.

## Challenge concentration, 2025 finalists

| Challenge | Finalists |
|---|---|
| Stellar Stories: Space Weather Through the Eyes of Earthlings | 8 |
| Will It Rain On My Parade? | 6 |
| A World Away: Hunting for Exoplanets with AI | 5 |
| Commercializing Low Earth Orbit (LEO) | 4 |
| Build a Space Biology Knowledge Engine | 4 |
| everything else | 1-2 each |

The top five challenges produced 27 of 45 finalists. These were also among the most
popular challenges, so this is partly volume. The useful signal is that popular
challenges are not penalized - three 2025 winners came from Commercializing LEO alone,
and in 2023 two different teams won on the same challenge.

## What loses

- A model with a headline accuracy and no baseline, no confusion matrix, no leakage check
- "Helps people make better decisions" with no named person
- A project page that describes the stack instead of the outcome
- A demo that requires the judge to invent their own input
- Broken or permission-gated links
- Drifting from the challenge's stated objectives because the team found something more
  interesting. Relevance is a full fifth of the score and it is the easiest to lose by
  accident.
