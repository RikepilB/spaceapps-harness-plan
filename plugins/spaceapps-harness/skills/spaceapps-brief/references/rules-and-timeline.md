# Rules, timeline and submission mechanics

## Registration

1. Create or log into a NASA Space Apps account at spaceappschallenge.org
2. Complete the registration form on the current year's hackathon page
3. Choose a Local Event (in-person or virtual) or the Universal Event
4. Accept Terms & Conditions

Registration stays open through the end of the hackathon. There is no cost.

## Event types

**In-person local event.** Judged by that event's local leads and judges. Smaller
judging pools mean a smaller field to beat for nomination, but local leads typically
nominate only a couple of projects.

**Virtual local event.** Same judging structure, run remotely.

**Universal Event.** For participants with no local option, or who prefer it. Judged by
Universal Event judges. In 2025 the Universal Event produced 9 of the 45 global
finalists and 2 of the 23 honorable mentions - the single largest source of finalists,
ahead of every individual country. It also produced two global winners (Astro Sweepers,
Galactic Impact; HerCode Space, Best Use of Storytelling).

## Team rules

- Maximum six members
- All members registered for the same event
- One team per person; one challenge per team
- Teams can form and change up until the hackathon closes
- Solo entry is permitted

## The no-early-work rule

Teams may not begin working on the challenges before the hackathon starts. What is
permitted before the event:

- Reading published challenge statements and their Resources lists
- Creating accounts (Earthdata Login, api.nasa.gov key, GitHub, hosting)
- Installing tooling and learning libraries
- Forming the team and agreeing roles
- Studying prior years' winners

What is not permitted: writing the project's code, building its assets, or producing
its models ahead of time.

## Submission

Projects are submitted through the team's project page on spaceappschallenge.org,
according to the closing time of the team's own local event. The year's Project
Submission Guide - published shortly before the hackathon - carries the authoritative
field list and any video length limit. Check it rather than assuming last year's rules.

Standing requirements across years:

- English
- Named challenge, exactly one
- A description of the project, how it works, and the data used
- Links to the demo and to the source
- Attribution for any third-party assets, with licences
- For physical or hardware builds: photographs or video plus enough description for
  someone else to recreate the work

## Practical failure modes

- Links that require login or are set to private. Test every link in a private window.
- Submitting at the deadline and hitting a slow upload. Submit a complete draft early
  and revise it.
- Confusing the local event's closing time with a global deadline. The local clock is
  the one that counts.
- A demo hosted on a machine that gets closed and carried home.
