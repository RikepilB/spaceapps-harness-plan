# The video and the pitch

Presentation is one of five criteria, and it multiplies the other four: a judge who does
not understand the project cannot score its Impact or Validity at all.

Check the year's submission guide for the video length limit and respect it exactly.
Over-length is a compliance problem, not a style problem.

## The 30-second structure

| Time | Beat | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5s | The stake | A specific person facing a specific decision with bad information. A scene, not an abstraction |
| 5-10s | The turn | The data that exists and is not reaching them. Name the instrument or mission out loud |
| 10-22s | The thing | The product on screen, doing the actual work, with real data visible |
| 22-27s | The proof | One number or one comparison, stated with its limitation |
| 27-30s | The horizon | Who uses this next week, and the project name |

Twelve of the thirty seconds are the product on screen. That ratio is the point.

## Rules

- Product on screen for more than half the runtime. Talking heads and title cards are
  the most common waste of a short video.
- Name the datasets aloud. "TEMPO nitrogen dioxide" is credibility; "NASA data" is noise.
- Subtitles always. Judges are international and many watch muted.
- Every claim must already be cleared. Re-recording costs an hour you will not have.
- No stock rocket footage. NASA judges have seen rockets.
- If a minor is on the team, they cannot appear or be named. Plan the shot list around
  that from the start, not in the edit.
- Record at hour 26-28, not hour 35. Encoding and uploading take longer than you think.

## Live demo run-of-show

Write it as a numbered sequence with timings and two named people: a driver who controls
the screen and a narrator who talks. Never the same person under pressure if you can
avoid it.

Include:
- The exact click path, using the pre-seeded known-good example
- One deliberate pause where you stop talking and let the screen work
- The failure plan: a screen recording of the working flow, queued in a second tab.
  A broken API then costs five seconds instead of the pitch

## Three pitch lengths

| Length | Contains | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| 30 seconds | Stake, thing, proof | A judge walking past |
| 2 minutes | Adds method and beneficiary | Standard local judging slot |
| 7 minutes | Adds data provenance, validation, limitations, roadmap | Rounds with Q&A |

All three open with the same sentence. Write that sentence first and make it carry the
whole project.

## The three questions

Prepare a one-sentence answer to each before you present:

1. "Where does this data come from and how current is it?"
2. "How do you know it's right?" - the answer is a baseline and a limitation, not a
   confidence claim
3. "Who uses this, and what stops them?"

A team that answers these three crisply reads as a team that understands its own project.
